# Voice: Jonathan → neighbours and the public (Nextdoor, the members site, anything a neighbour reads)

Source: his 3 September 2026 Nextdoor reply on street trees, posted verbatim at `HOA/16_NeighborInteractions/Nextdoor/20260903_Nextdoor_street_trees/POSTED_reply_to_lariscy.md`, and his own shorter post above it in the same thread. Written 2026-09-03 by Claude (LP) after drafting this badly twice.

## The stance, in his words

**Humble, approachable, credible.** All three at once, and the order is load-bearing. Humble is what makes the credibility land, because a neighbour who feels lectured stops reading. Approachable is what makes a stranger reply. Credible is the citation, the link, the date, the quotation.

This is not the contractor texting register. Do not reach for `casual-contractor-and-text.md` because the audience is informal. He does not say "howdy" or "'em" or "folks ought to" in public. His own words for what that felt like: "Why the fuck are you folksy?"

## Structure

Answer first, then the evidence, with an explicit break between them.

He opens with the finding in two short paragraphs and closes them with "So, there you have it." Then a header, "#Here's the much longer version#", and everything else below it. A reader who quits after twenty seconds still leaves knowing the answer. Never run a chronology and let the conclusion assemble itself.

## The moves that make it work

**Give the other side the exit.** "I'm honestly not sure if the board is aware of their own tree responsibilities." Generous and unanswerable together. They can take the out or explain themselves, and either is fine.

**Answer the obvious objection before anyone raises it.** On why the covenants mention a strip you do not own: "I think the reason the planting strip is mentioned at all is because it's not part of our deeded property, so the covenants make sure we mow it."

**Hedge next to the sharpest claim, never instead of it.** "(not a lawyer)", "though again, I could be wrong". The claim stays; the tone comes down.

**Say what you are tired of.** "rather than answers like 'the board decided,' which I'm kind of tired of hearing, to be honest. (Hence the website.)" One clause explains the entire project. Do not sand the feeling out.

**Ask for something.** He cannot attend, so he recruits: "if anyone is willing to ask in the room, I'd love to hear the answer and read it in the next set of meeting minutes." That turns readers into participants and puts a non-answer on the record.

**Disclaim the instruction.** "I'm not telling anybody what to do with the tree in front of their own house." Said before the part that is obviously advice.

## Register markers

- Contractions throughout. Plain words. Short sentences next to long ones, unevenly.
- Dry asides in parentheses: "(coolest title ever, right?)", "(the city does)", "(Hence the website.)"
- "However, (!)" — his own emphasis punctuation.
- ALL CAPS on one word for stress: "the POA must REPLACE".
- Em-dashes stay. See the cross-register DNA note in the humanizer skill.
- Warmth arrives through an aside or a joke, never through dialect.
- He reuses his own good lines. The Urban Forester joke was in his April email to Stroud before it was in the post. Read his prior writing to the same person before drafting.

## Credibility mechanics

Link the source. Quote it verbatim, including the clause that cuts against him. Give the date he did each thing and the date he asked the Board. Name the ordinance by number. Never characterise anyone's conduct as unlawful or deliberate.

**Address the office, not the person.** His rule, 2026-09-03. He writes "the President of the Board mentioned" and "a board member proposed replacing them" where the minutes give both names. The duty attaches to the office, so naming the human turns a governance question into a neighbourhood argument and hands the reader a side to take.

Two limits on it. A verbatim quotation of the minutes or the general ledger keeps whatever names it contains, because that is the Association's own record of an official act and redacting a citation costs more credibility than the name costs restraint. And the substitution needs an office to substitute: a management company employee has none, so she is named. Jonathan on that: "Tracy has to be named... She has no office." Replacing her with "the management company's representative" is vaguer without being more restrained.

## Don't

- Don't formalise it. This is not the attorney register.
- Don't make it folksy. That is the contractor register and it reads as performance in public.
- Don't write it colder than he is. That is the standing failure recorded in `HOA/00_WorkProduct/DRAFT_VS_SENT.md`.
- Don't bury the finding in a timeline.
