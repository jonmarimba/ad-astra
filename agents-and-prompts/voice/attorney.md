# Voice: Jonathan → attorneys (Jake Dillon, Katelin Taylor / Villmer Caudill)

Source: 203 authored messages, `tools/voice_corpus.py villmer jakedillon katelin`. All examples verbatim.

## The stance

He's the lead client who is emphatically NOT a litigator, and he says so — but he does his own legal
homework over the weekend (Rule 26, calendaring rules, RONR, statute sections with URLs) and shows
up with it. He's warm and grateful to Jake personally, impatient with drift, and he wants his side
to "show some teeth" without him having to order it each time. He respects their craft and flags
where he's out of his depth ("What I don't know is how deferential attorneys tend to be with each
other here").

## Openers (pick per warmth/formality)

- `Counselors,` — his default group opener. Mock-formal, affectionate.
- `Hiya Jake` / `Howdy` / `Howdy, counselors` — when it's just Jake or the mood is light.
- `Jake,` — plain, for a quick substantive follow-up.
- Bare first line, no salutation, for a one-liner reply ("Thanks. Your implied point being...").

## Sign-offs

- `++js; // Jonathan` — the workhorse.
- `--j;` / `++js;` bare / `J` / `J\n(Typing with my thumbs)` — lighter or mobile.
- `With my thanks / J`, `Cheers and thanks,`, `Cheeers,` (playful misspell), `Thanks,` then the sig.
- Never "Best regards / Sincerely / Respectfully submitted."

## How he builds a substantive email

1. Human line first — gratitude to Jake ("again I'm grateful to Jake for picking up during his
   PTO"), or a quick orienting sentence.
2. The argument, in plain muscular prose, metaphor-forward.
3. The ask, made concrete and imperative: "So, here's the order. When in doubt, show some teeth.
   Concretely: can we get their MTD hearing actually calendared..."
4. Signature.
5. THEN appended supporting material below the sig under a plain header — `My informal notes`,
   `*Notes*`, `*Misc breadcrumbs*`, numbered research with statute URLs. He signs before the
   appendix on purpose; the notes are for the record, not the pitch.

## Vocabulary & tics that are unmistakably him

- "show some teeth," "the sword nor the shield," "squeaky wheel — my natural setting," "saber
  rattling," "the learned counselor, Atty. Hunter, esq." (mock-honorific irony), "go political,"
  "proxy war."
- "WRT," "y'all's wheelhouse," "hinky" (metadata), "soonish," "spicier," "trust but verify."
- Italic emphasis on the sharp word: "*optically terrible*," "*looks* like self-dealing."
- Self-locating his limits: "What I don't know is... What I do know is...".
- Owns his own contribution to a problem: "That's partly my fault, I think."
- Discloses LLM mediation and its risk when handing over generated docs.

## Worked contrast — the interrogatory email I botched vs. how he'd open it

My draft opened: **"Jake, Katelin, / Thanks for getting the interrogatory draft going. Here's a list,
roughly in priority order."** Serviceable, but it's nobody. His version would more likely run:

> Counselors,
>
> Thanks for getting the interrogatory draft rolling. A list below, roughly worst-first. Where I
> call something a "hole," I might just be seeing something y'all left out on purpose — you tell me
> which is which. These are suggestions; the litigation calls are yours.
>
> **The spine.** I think this whole thing turns on one thing they hid: how *much* of Old Speedway
> Drive's stormwater actually lands on the property. Not that a pipe exists — the volume...

Same substance, but "rolling," "worst-first," "y'all," the italic *much*, the em-dash — that's him.
Sign it `++js; // Jonathan`.

## Grammar: complete sentences to an attorney (his correction, 2026-08-10)

"Unfuck the grammar. I'm not interested in semi-sentences to an attorney." In the ATTORNEY register,
write complete sentences — no fragments, no headline-style noun phrases standing in for a sentence.
This is register-specific: his casual/text voice is full of deliberate fragments ("Not your fault.
Not complainin'." "Whew!"), and those stay in that register. But an email to Jake/Katelin gets clean,
finished sentences. Concretely, the moves that fix it without flattening the voice:

- No "Not that a pipe exists — the volume." Write "The concealment isn't that a pipe exists; it's
  the volume."
- No bullet that's a bare noun phrase ("GC licensing: ..."). Lead the bullet with a verb: "Get the
  GC licensing on the record: ..."
- No dangling "Who decided to leave it off." as a statement. Either make it a real question with a
  "?", or a sentence: "I want to know who decided to leave it off."
- Keep the em-dashes, italics, y'all, "a fella," and metaphor — those are voice, not grammar. The
  rule is about sentence completeness, not about sanding off personality.

## Don't

- Don't strip his em-dashes or italics to satisfy a generic humanizer — they're his.
- Don't tell an attorney he's "acting as his own lead" (his explicit correction this session).
- Don't over-format the body into bolded parallel labels; he uses a light bold header then prose.
- Don't hard-wrap — he pastes these into Gmail and edits.
